Who judges meaning?

Semantic entropy needs a judge of when two answers mean the same thing. Today that judge sits outside the model. We want it inside.

20 Oct 2026 · 3 min

Part 1 ended on a distinction. The entropy of the next token measures doubt about wording: it runs high wherever a sentence can go on in several equivalent ways. The doubt we want is about meaning.

Semantic entropy

The measure for doubt about meaning already exists: semantic entropy (Kuhn, Gal and Farquhar, 2023). Sample several answers to the same question. Group the ones that mean the same thing. Take the entropy over the groups instead of over the tokens. Ten wordings of one answer make one group, and no doubt; three different answers make three groups, and real doubt.

Everything rests on the grouping, and the grouping rests on one test: do two answers mean the same?

Why the test is bidirectional

The test comes from natural language inference. An NLI model reads a premise and a hypothesis and decides whether the first entails the second. Entailment runs one way. Paris entails a city in France; a city in France does not entail Paris.

So the test is run both ways. If each answer entails the other, neither says more than the other, and in logic that is equivalence:

(A ⇒ B) ∧ (B ⇒ A) ≡ (A ⇔ B)

Paris and the capital is Paris pass in both directions and share a group. Paris and a city in France pass in one and stay apart.

The judge sits outside

In the original method the test is run by a separate model, an NLI classifier; the 2024 follow-up in Nature also used a prompted language model. Either way, a second model reads the first one's samples and decides which of them mean the same.

The doubt is the model's, but the judgement of what its answers mean belongs to someone else.

Where we are

That is the current state of the work: moving the judge inside. The model that wrote the answers should be the one that decides when two of them are equivalent. This log records the problem as it stands; the approach and results come in the next part.
